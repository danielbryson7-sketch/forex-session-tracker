How this study works. The 9:30, 9:45, 10:00 and 10:15 a.m. New York candles set an opening range. Its high and low are fixed when the fourth candle closes at 10:30. Low before high draws a bullish fib; high before low draws a bearish fib. If both extremes are in one candle, its body direction breaks the tie because OHLC cannot reveal their order. The 62–79% OTE lines begin at 10:30 and run to the session close. Daily Supertrend still selects trade direction, so it can disagree with the fib orientation. An OTE touch and rejection arms a setup when EMA20/50 align and ATR14/ATR50 is at most 1.4; a later 70.5% touch enters. Entries run through the 3:30 p.m. candle, with at most two per day and one open position. The favorable opening-range extreme sets the target. There is no stop loss; remaining trades close at 4 p.m. Results are QQQ price points, not option-contract returns. This is a historical OHLC simulation: candle order is approximated, and spread, slippage, fees and executable fills are not included. No orders are placed.
